### Setting up the Content Brief
So this is a good example. This is an actual conversation I had that led to a finished article that we've published — a guide to Python for SEO. The process starts: I paste in a content brief as the very first step. The way I see a lot of people use AI is they create an article and they spend a bunch of time post-hoc editing the article. I don't think that's a good process because there's a limit to how much you can polish a finished draft. So this process tries to frontload all of the input and direction and guidance right at the very start, and then get out of the way and let the AI generate the article based on that.

First thing in the brief: target keyword — these are keyword targeted articles. Then working title. I always think this is really important because you're setting the end goal of what you want to arrive at. In this case, "how to get started with Python for SEO." Then key points to include. This is where I say from my own knowledge what are the things I expect to see in this finished article. This is why this is not a cheap process that anyone could do, because it's dependent on having a sense of the conclusion you want to arrive at. So I said my motivation for writing this was that Python can help automate things, it's a good starting point for more technical skills, and also mention that I have experience using this Python course. I wanted to cover the core concepts needed to actually use Python, recommend some simple Python projects, and I thought Patrick from our team knows tons about Python, he's got loads of cool scripts, let's have a section where we include that.

And then to help it rank, what I do is I run an AI Content Helper report for every target keyword. I copy-paste the recommended topics that it identifies in there as optional context for the LLM to include. For those who don't know what AI Content Helper is, it's a tool in Ahrefs — you feed it a keyword, it pulls the top ranking search results and pulls the topics from those pages and gives you a topical map of what the top ranking results are covering. And then last thing, relevant Ahrefs products/features to reference. ### The Importance of Human Review
I was watching a talk by Andrej Karpathy — his ideology is that no AI output should be published without a human reviewing it first. If there's a system that generates a thousand banner ads, it's not sustainable for a human to review all thousand, so AI should generate things only in quantities that people can review, because it still hallucinates. So it makes sense that whenever you generate an AI article, especially under your own name, you review it start to finish. Yeah, especially when it's published under my name, I feel a sense of ownership. People expect a certain level of quality and I don't want to mislead people.

### A Closer Look at the 7 Foundational Documents
The starting prompt is the same as the instructions. I write all the individual documents in my notes in markdown and export them as a folder and dump them into ChatGPT. We discussed it makes sense to tell it to go step by step and not move between steps until you approve. Content brief — the template I use, a quick way to add the information the LLM needs. Outline — some of the projects: the AI does a good job, works to a checklist of editing principles. MECE is probably the most important framework. Pyramid principle — stick to one idea per section, support it with evidence, then offer additional context (popularized by Barbara Minto, former McKinsey consultant). Is each section appropriately weighted relative to total word count? Make sure the most important ideas have the greatest word count. Do we deliver on what was promised in the title? Do the headers provide clear benefits and explicit advice?

How to write for Ahrefs — the guidelines plus examples. How to mention Ahrefs products and features — we aim to educate generally but we also showcase Ahrefs as part of the solution when it makes sense, in a natural way. How to line edit an article draft — final copy editing. Open each section with a core idea (BLUF). Have we addressed the most obvious objections? Have we provided sufficient evidence? Use dense words — "novel" instead of "something new." Define any jargon. Use parallel formats for headers. Is the sentence structure varied to sound pleasing? (Gary Provost "writing is music" quote.) Have we included first person anecdotes and replaced phrases that sound like AI — "This isn't X, it's Y," "It's more than X, it's Y," "Their secret."

Internal links — I say read through this article, find 10 relevant places to integrate existing Ahrefs blog content as a link. It does it contextually, but about half of the links are completely hallucinated and made up. I had Mel message me about weird broken links appearing in recent articles — I published hallucinated URLs. So I could export a list of real blog post URLs maybe with an AI summary and ask it to pick from that list. To be honest this can be done after the article is published — in Ahrefs Site Audit we have a link opportunities report that automatically finds which keywords you can link to which articles.

Metadata — meta description and URL slug, very easy, saves me thought. And the last stage, adding WordPress short codes. This is still something that takes the team a ton of time; we're quite close to this being solved.
